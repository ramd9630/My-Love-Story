Chapter 18: The Driver’s 11th Mile 😂

23 February 2017

Some mornings begin with a simple good morning. And before you know it, you end up discussing a lorry, a movie dialogue, and the day’s song.

That was one of our mornings.

At 7:08 AM, Mahechuu sent me a message.

Good morning nityaaaaaaaaaa

I replied at 8:36 AM, with a good morning of my own. But a normal reply was never enough when it came to us. I started spelling her name, one letter at a time.

M

A

H

E

C

H

U

U

U

And so on.

She responded by stretching my name just as much.

Nityaaaaaaaaaaaawaaawawww

Heyyyy

Chudale

Sry

Nityaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaa

Pettesavaaa

We were already busy calling each other by our favourite names when Mahechuu dropped the real news.

Telusaaaa just miss lekapotey lorry ki pettesedunu driver

I stopped playing around for a moment.

Babu

Podhunne

Alaantivi

Cheppaku

It was still early in the morning, and this was not exactly the kind of update I wanted to hear from her. She followed it up with:

Sudden break vesadu

I could imagine how quickly an ordinary journey could turn into a frightening moment. But Mahechuu was still there, messaging me, and within a minute, the conversation was back to its usual rhythm.

That was the thing about us. Even when a conversation took an unexpected turn, we somehow found our way back to teasing each other.

At 8:42 AM, Mahechuu brought up Businessman.

Niku business man mvi lo last mahesh oka dialogue cheputadu gurtunda

I asked her what it was.

Ni target at 11th mile ani

Then she added another line:

And evaru mata asalu vinaku manishi mata asalu vinaku ani

And suddenly, the driver became the star of our morning conversation.

Adi follow avutunadu emo ma driver

Asalu entha fast ga tesukuvelutunado

I burst out laughing.

Hahahaha

Ayyooo

Malokam

Mahechuu had managed to connect a real-life ride to a movie dialogue, and once she made that connection, the whole thing became funnier. The driver was apparently taking the Businessman philosophy a little too seriously.

Then, just like that, she changed the subject.

Nityaaaaaaaa

Eroju em whistle?

This was another familiar part of our conversations: the sudden shift from one topic to another, without any formal transition.

I told her I was going to my village and that I didn't know.

E roju uru

Gng

So

Don't know

But I still had a song in mind.

Oka vela unte... Kanuka... Adhi manmadhudu mvi

She liked the choice.

Ohhh avuna

Heyy super

Of course, I couldn't leave a perfectly good moment without teasing her again. I sent a few more messages, and she admitted:

Edigo nv em anav adi asalu ardamkavdam ledhu

I laughed at that, too.

By then, Mahechuu told me she would call and explain later. She was almost at the ground.

Inka 5 mins lo ground

A few minutes later, she messaged again.

Clz vachesindi

And our morning conversation slowly came to a stop as she reached college.

Looking back at this exchange, what stands out is not just the near miss with the lorry or the Businessman reference. It is how naturally we moved from concern to jokes, from movie dialogues to songs, and from one random thought to the next.

There was no need for a big occasion or a carefully planned conversation. We could start with a good morning, spend several messages stretching each other's names, turn a driver's speed into a movie joke, and end up discussing a song before college.

That morning, even an ordinary journey gave us something to laugh about. And sometimes, those are the conversations that stay with you.
