Chapter 17 — A Cup of Tea and a Thousand Candles

There was some work in Ashok and Roshni’s team, and the TL had asked Guru and me to help out.

I was excited at first. New work meant a chance to learn something new.

I went to Ashok and asked, “What’s the task?”

He explained, “Just enter the required details in each field and take a screenshot of the result. You need to do that for 300 records.”

That was it.

Whatever excitement I had disappeared the moment I heard the task.

But there was no other option. I had to do it.

So, I got started.

**Alt + Tab. Ctrl + C. Ctrl + V. Ctrl + S. Repeat.**

I finished it at a pretty good pace. It was the kind of work that required more patience than skill, and I was running low on the former.

**4:30 PM.**

I had just finished my tea and returned to my desk.

Boredom kicked in again.

I picked up the desktop landline and called Amma.

She was having her second round of evening tea.

We spoke for two or three minutes before she said, “Fine, I’m hanging up.”

But I didn’t want to end the call just yet.

“Amma, edhokati matlaadu… bore koduthundhi.”

*Mom, talk about something… I’m bored.*

She laughed.

“Haha! Era, antha khaali ga unnava?”

*Haha! Boy, are you really that free?*

“Emo ma… bore koduthundhi. Edhoti matlaadu.”

*I don’t know, Mom… I’m bored. Just talk about something.*

“Em matlaadanu ra? Maaku kuda daily boring gaane undhi ikkada. Nanna garu, nenu… ala gadipesthunnam rojulu, nuvvu annayya vellipoyina daggara nunchi.”

*What should I talk about, dear? It’s boring for us here every day too. Your father and I are just passing our days like this ever since you and your brother left.*

“Mmm… avunu. Ardham avuthundhi.”

*Hmm… yeah. I understand.*

“Inkenti mari?”

*What else, then?*

“Cheppali…”

*I have to tell you something…*

“Eppudu vasthunnav?”

*When are you coming home?*

“Adagaali… adigi chebuthaa. Appudu ticket book chesukunta.”

*I have to ask for permission first. I’ll let you know, and then I’ll book the ticket.*

“Sare… nuvvu chebithe annayya ki kuda chebuthaa rammani. Oka mokku undhi… theerchukovali.”

*Okay. Once you confirm, I’ll tell your brother to come too. There’s a vow I need to fulfil.*

“Ekkada?”

*Where?*

“Seethapalli Bapanamma gudi lo.”

*At the Seethapalli Bapanamma temple.*

“Edhi… Maredumilli velle dhaarilo untundhi adha?”

*The one on the way to Maredumilli?*

“Mmm… adhe.”

*Hmm… that one.*

“Saree, aithe undu. Colleague pilusthunnadu. Malli chesthaa.”

*Okay, then. Hold on, a colleague is calling me. I’ll call you again.*

“Tea aindha?”

*Finished your tea?*

“Haa, aindhi. Untane.”

*Yes, finished. Bye.*

I hung up.

Ashok called out to me.

“Datta, mudinjircha?”

*Datta, have you finished?*

“Illa, Ashok. 50–60% done.”

*Mmm, okay. Leave it. Don’t work on it anymore.*

I was confused.

“Ennachi, Ashok?”

*What happened, Ashok?*

“Arun automated it,” he said.

“Arey yaar!”

I said it in pain.

All that repetitive work, all those screenshots, all those Ctrl+C and Ctrl+V cycles… and now I was being told to stop because someone had automated the whole thing.

I stared at my screen, wondering why I hadn’t been told earlier.

**5:30 PM.**

Chechi called.

“Dattu, free for tea?”

Why would I ever say no to that? Silly Chechi.

*Anything for her,* I thought.

We went for tea.

“So, para, Dattu. What’s bothering you? Why were you upset over the weekend? And why didn’t you come out with us for the beach?”

She got straight to the point.

I stirred the tea in my glass, playing with what little was left.

“Chechi… Maheshuu said she got an alliance. And when I heard it, I couldn’t handle it.”

“I see. What’s between the two of you?”

She asked without even a second thought.

Not that she needed to think twice before asking me something like that. She had every right to.

Chechi was one of those people who stood close to me, Guru, and Venki. She knew us well enough to see when something was bothering us, even when we tried to hide it.

She was on the bench at the time, having left the project because of internal politics.

Now, with no project work to distract her, she seemed determined to get to the bottom of my problem.

I knew there was no point in trying to dodge her questions.

“Chechi, I don’t want to lie to you. I really like her. But at the same time, marriage and all that won’t happen. So why should I continue?”

She looked at me.

“Ohhh! So, is she from a different caste?”

“No,” I replied, nodding.

“Hmm… which caste is considered higher?”

She had stepped into a sensitive topic, but I answered her anyway.

*(Sensored for you.)*

“Then what’s the problem?” she asked.

“Arey, Chechi! Her father is like Hitler. And I… OMG! I can’t imagine facing situations like that in my life. Going to her house and asking her parents for their daughter’s hand? How silly is that? What a pointless thing to do!”

I said it as though the very thought of it was exhausting.

“Arey, Dattu… you have to do such things if you want someone in your life.”

“Neek enti, Chechi… emaina chebuthaav!”

*Easy for you to say, Chechi. You can say anything!*

“Entha? Endha?”

She looked confused, unable to understand what I had just said.

“Nothing,” I replied, deliberately bringing the discussion to an end. “So, that’s the reason I didn’t come out during the weekend.”

“Hmm… so, all good now?”

She clearly wasn’t ready to let the topic go.

“Yeah, Chechi. For now… alliance cancelled.”

The moment I said those words, **a thousand candles lit up in my eyes.**

“Ooooooh!” she exclaimed in her Malayalam accent.

Then she continued, “So, Dattu is talking to her again?”

I nodded.

“Hmm… better confess your feelings to her and see her response. Otherwise, you’ll be in trouble.”

And just like that, she ended the conversation.

But her words stayed with me.

**Confess your feelings.**

Until then, I had somehow managed to keep my feelings tucked away in a corner of my heart, convincing myself that there was no point in pursuing something that had no future.

Now, Chechi had put a new thought in my head.

What if I told Maheshuu how I felt?

What if I didn’t?

For the first time that evening, the boredom was gone.

In its place was anxiety.

A different kind of anxiety.

The kind that comes when someone makes you consider doing the very thing you’ve been avoiding.

Thankfully, Chechi moved on to another topic before I could get lost in those thoughts. She started talking about her upcoming weekend trip home.

When she asked about my plans, I told her, “I’ll also go home during the same weekend, Chechi.”

Lately, she had become one of the reasons I made sure to visit home every two months without fail.

And as usual, we planned to meet at the roadside pani puri stall.

Another weekend.

Another meeting.

And somewhere in the middle of all this, a confession I wasn’t sure I was ready to make.
